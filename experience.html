Data Analytics and Business Intelligence at Softex

  1. Home

At Softex I worked on data and research projects about the Brazilian ICT sector, innovation, employability, entrepreneurship and regional development.

The data was rarely ready to use. Most of the work was turning fragmented, heterogeneous and sometimes poorly structured information into reliable outputs for reports, dashboards and decisions.

From raw data to dashboards

Most projects followed the same three steps, from the data source to the final dashboard:

CollectGather data from public and administrative sources (part of it pulled from BigQuery with SQL) and primary survey data from Airtable.
IntegrateCombine, clean and validate the sources in Google Sheets
ReportConnect the integrated data to Power BI dashboards

Keeping these steps separate made each update easier to repeat and to check, and kept the dashboards tied to validated data.

Heterogeneous data sources

I collected and analysed secondary data on ICT markets, innovation, R&D, startups, internationalisation, employment and economic indicators.

The sources differed in structure, terminology, definitions and completeness, so validating and standardising them came before any analysis. For each source I checked how a variable had been generated, what it actually measured, and whether two sources could fairly be compared or combined.

Indicators and analytical datasets

I built indicators for programmes such as PPEI/PPI and other Softex initiatives. Each one needed an explicit aggregation logic, a well-defined numerator and denominator, and a check for inconsistencies, so that it could be reproduced and explained.

This is where I started separating raw data, processed data and datasets prepared for analysis.

Data integration and record linkage

For the ET 4.0 analyses I integrated employment data from CAGED and RAIS, Brazil's two main administrative labour datasets.

They differ in structure, time periods, classifications, identifiers and granularity. Joining them consistently gave me hands-on experience with data integration and record linkage, and with the limits of administrative data.

Survey analytics: Conecta Startup Brasil

Conecta Startup Brasil is a federal programme, run by Softex with the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation, ABDI and CNPq, that supports early-stage startups in all five Brazilian regions through training, mentoring, non-repayable grants and partnerships with established companies. I analysed its primary survey data to produce the evidence the federal government relied on when deciding whether to continue funding the programme.

I structured the analysis around the programme's own objectives. Segmenting by region tested whether support was reaching beyond Brazil's traditional innovation hubs. Business model and B2B/B2C orientation showed whether partnerships with large companies were turning into real market traction. Company size revealed where the support made the most difference. These breakdowns exposed patterns that the overall averages concealed.

I translated the findings into clear, decision-ready reports for policymakers without a statistical background, setting out what was working, where the programme fell short and what remained uncertain.

What changed in how I work

Over time I moved from producing reports to asking better questions before touching the data:

  • What question are we trying to answer?
  • Is this data appropriate for answering it?
  • How should it be transformed?
  • Which comparisons or segments are meaningful?
  • What can we legitimately conclude?

This role gave me experience across the whole analytical process, from data acquisition and preparation to analysis, visualisation and communication.
